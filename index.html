The problem

School tracks your homework. Nothing tracks the rest.

Every assignment has a due date, a reminder and a grade. Piano, robotics, running, your job, your applications — those have nobody checking. So they're the first thing to slip, and the hardest to get back.

Cadence gives the rest of your life the same structure school gives homework: a plan, a nudge, and someone who notices.

How it works

Four steps, about a minute.

Tell it what you're working toward

Piano five times a week. Robotics Monday and Wednesday. Essays done before November. Pick from tiles and Cadence fills in sensible times.

It pulls your homework from Canvas

Assignments, due dates and instructions come in automatically through the Chrome extension — no copying anything over.

You get a plan that fits your actual week

Cadence works out how long each assignment takes, then schedules it and your practice around school, practice and everything already on your calendar.

Check in, once a day

One tap after you practice. Your ring fills, your streak grows, your crew sees it, and your activities record gets another line.

What you get

A planner that knows the difference between due and done.

Realistic time estimates

It reads the assignment — word counts, problem counts, rubrics — and learns how long things actually take you, class by class.

Streaks that forgive

Show up once a day and the streak grows. Miss a day and a freeze covers it, because one bad day shouldn't erase a month.

Your crew

Partners see your weekly rings and can nudge you. Clubs and teams share one goal. You choose what's shared — the rest never leaves your device.

Assignment guides

What you're actually doing, in plain words. A turn-in checklist, the steps in order, and links to every file and page you'll need.

One week, one grid

Homework, practice, shifts and class all on the same calendar, so you can see the free evening before you promise it away.

A Sunday reset

Two minutes: what you hit, what slipped, and a smaller target when twice in a row was too much. Consistency beats intensity.

The part future-you cares about

Your activities list writes itself.

Colleges ask for hours per week, weeks per year, your role, and 150 characters describing what you did. Most students reconstruct that from memory in senior fall.

Every check-in you make is one row of that answer. Cadence keeps the hours, the streaks and the notes, and hands them back when you need them.
