    Nobody else keeps track of your activities

    Canvas lists every assignment, and teachers chase late work. Nobody reminds you that the robotics build log is due to your mentor Friday, or that you said you’d practice piano five days this week. That part is on you, and it’s the first thing to slip.

    Cadence plans your activities in the same week as your homework.

    • Set times stay clear

      Add practice, a club or a job at set times, and Cadence never plans work on top of it.

    • Weekly goals get their own sessions

      Say piano five times a week, and Cadence finds the time alongside your homework.

    • Check-ins add up

      Tap Check in after practice. Your check-ins build a record of hours in the shape the Common App asks for.

    • Share it with a partner or your club

      Sign in to add an accountability partner or join your club. They see your check-ins for the activities you share, and they can send you a nudge.

    When plans change, just tell it

    Type it the way you’d say it. Cadence changes your plan and lists what it changed. One tap undoes it.

    Things you can say

    • “Practice is cancelled today”
    • “Dinner at 6:30 tonight”
    • “Spanish quiz Thursday, 30 min”
    • “I’m tired, an hour of work tonight”

    Something with a time goes on your calendar, and any work in its way is planned again. If you only say you’re tired, it asks how much you can do and offers the answers as buttons.

    The rest of your week goes in the same plan

    Homework, with time to do it
    Every assignment gets a time estimate. Tap Quicker, About right or Longer when you finish, and later estimates for that kind of work in that class adjust.
    Your calendars and email
    Paste your Canvas calendar feed link once, and a Google, Outlook or iCloud calendar link if you use one, and due dates and events keep coming in. In the Chrome extension, connect Gmail and the practices, games and meetings in your email land on your calendar, marked From email.
    On Canvas or on any screen
    The Chrome extension puts a Cadence button on every Canvas page and reads your assignments with the login you already have. The website is the same planner with more room, plus keyboard shortcuts.

    Sign in with the same email in the extension and on the website, and both show the same plan.

    How it decides when you work

    You don’t have to tell Cadence how many hours to work. It works that out from your calendar and what’s due, using four rules.

    1. It starts when you’re free and stops before bed

      On a school day, work can start 20 minutes after your last class. On other days it starts at least an hour and a half after you wake up. It always stops 30 minutes before bed, and everything on your calendar stays clear.

    2. No day gets heavier than it has to

      Cadence starts every school day at the lightest load that works and raises it only as far as it takes for everything to be done before it’s due.

    3. The next deadline comes first

      Cadence plans the most urgent work before anything else. Long assignments get split across several days, and you never get more than two blocks of the same thing back to back.

    4. Every assignment gets a plan, even in a packed week

      When a week is too full, some days get longer and, as a last resort, work is booked after its due date. Cadence tells you which days and assignments that affected, so you can decide what to drop or ask your teacher about.

    Where your information goes

    Cadence doesn’t show ads, records only anonymous use and never sells or rents anything about you. Here is what it keeps and who else gets it.

    • You can download your plan or delete it.

      In Account, Download my data saves it as a file you can keep. Delete my data erases it, or Delete account once you’ve signed in with an email.

    • Your crew sees only what you share.

      A partner or a club sees your name and your check-ins for the activities you choose: the activity, how long, how it went and any note. Nothing about your assignments or your calendar.

    • Your email stays on your computer.

      Gmail is read in the Chrome extension, on your computer. Your email isn’t sent to Cadence or to any AI company.

    • What you tell the assistant leaves your browser.

      Your message goes to the AI company that answers it, with a summary of your week, so don’t type anything private.

    Common questions

    Is Cadence free?

    Yes. There’s no paid plan and there are no ads.

    Does my school have to set anything up?

    No. On the website you paste your Canvas calendar feed link, and the Chrome extension reads Canvas with the login you already have in your browser. Either way there’s no API token to create and nothing for an administrator to install.

    Can I use it without Canvas?

    Yes. Type an assignment the way you’d say it, like “Spanish quiz Thursday, 30 min”, and Cadence plans it just like one from Canvas. Leave out the time and it estimates one. Activities and events don’t need Canvas at all. The sample week doesn’t use Canvas either, and it comes with a week already filled in.

    How does it know how long something takes?

    It starts from the assignment’s title and instructions. When you finish, tap Quicker, About right or Longer, and later estimates for that kind of work in that class adjust. You can also set the time yourself on any assignment.

    What is in the app?

    Five tabs. Today shows what to do next. Calendar has everything with a time on it. School lists every assignment, broken into steps. Activities covers what you do outside class, with a check-in after each one. Crew is for accountability partners and clubs. The sample week has all five.

    What if I am having a bad day?

    Tell the assistant how much you can do tonight, and the rest of tonight’s work moves to other days. If those days are already full, Cadence either goes past your number or plans work after its due date, and it tells you which one it did so you can choose what happens.

    Does it work on my phone?

    Yes. The website works in your phone’s browser, and your Canvas assignments come in through your Canvas calendar feed link. The Chrome extension, which can also read points and what you’ve submitted, runs in Chrome on a computer or a Chromebook.

    Who can see my plan?

    No other person, unless you add an accountability partner or join a club. Even then, they only see your name and your check-ins for the activities you chose to share, and nothing about your assignments or your calendar. When you use the assistant, a summary of your week goes with your message to the AI company that answers it. The privacy policy says exactly what other people can see.

    Is the assistant an AI?

    On a copy of Cadence that offers it, yes. Your messages go to Gemini, an AI model made by Google, which may use them to improve its products and may have people read them, so don’t type anything private. The assistant changes your plan through the same steps you could take yourself, and if you add your own Anthropic key, Claude does the same job. If neither can be reached, ordinary code on your own device reads simple sentences. The privacy policy lists exactly what is sent.

    Can Cadence read my email?

    Only if you connect Gmail in the Chrome extension. Then, while Cadence is open, it reads your recent email on your computer, with read-only access, finds practices, games, deadlines and changes, and adds them to your calendar labelled From email, with the sentence each came from. Practices, games and meetings with a time get planned around. A deadline shows up on its day, and Cadence plans time for it once you add it as an assignment. Your email isn’t sent to Cadence or to any AI company, and the events stay on your device. Delete any that are wrong, switch it off or disconnect at any time. Where your information goes shows what is kept and who receives it, and the privacy policy explains exactly what happens.

    Can Cadence see my Google Calendar?

    Only if you connect it. Paste your calendar’s secret link, and Cadence’s server fetches it for you and keeps nothing. Or, in the Chrome extension, sign in with Google and it reads the calendars you pick. Only if you turn it on does it add its own work blocks to one calendar you choose, and it never changes an event it didn’t make. Your events are kept on your device, and the assistant gets their times, without their titles, in its summary of your week.

    Who can use Cadence?

    Students who are 13 or older. If you’re under 18, check with a parent or guardian before you sign in with an email, and follow your school’s rules about the tools you use for schoolwork.
