The planner that schedules your homework.

Cadence reads your assignments from Canvas and plans the work around school, practice and sleep. When your day changes, tell it in a sentence.

Free, with no ads. Nothing for your school to set up.

Five tabs, one job each.

The same five as along the bottom of the app above. Try them there.

Today
What to do next, and your whole day as one bar from waking to bed: school, plans, activities and homework, each in its own colour.
Calendar
Everything with a time on it: school hours, practice, a dentist appointment, what is due, and the blocks planned for the work itself.
School
Every assignment, with how long it should take and the steps to finish it. Each step is tied to the block it is planned for.
Activities
Practice, teams, clubs, a job. Homework is planned around them, and a weekly count shows whether you showed up.
Crew
Accountability partners and clubs. They see check-ins for the activities you choose to share, and can send kudos or a nudge. Your assignments and calendar stay private.

It works out your hours from your calendar.

Most planners open with a settings page. Students do not know those numbers, and the same answer every Tuesday is wrong the Tuesday there is a meet.

Free time is worked out per day

A day runs from when you wake to when you sleep. School and your calendar take their hours. What is left is where work can go.

A light week stays light

Cadence tries the lightest evenings first and makes them heavier only when something would otherwise miss its due date.

What is due soonest goes first

Long assignments are split across several days, with no more than two blocks of the same thing back to back.

Everything gets a place

A full week is still planned in full. Cadence makes some days longer and, as a last resort, books work after its due date, then tells you which days and which assignments. You decide what to drop, move or ask your teacher about.

Say what is going on. The plan changes.

Type that you are home and tired. Cadence puts dinner on the calendar, keeps tonight to what you can do, and moves the rest.

  • Every reply lists what changed and what moved because of it.
  • One tap undoes the whole change.
  • Say “I am tired” with no number and it asks one question, with the answers as buttons.

A Chrome extension on Canvas, and this website.

On Canvas

The extension adds a Cadence button to every Canvas page and reads your courses and assignments with the login you already have there. There is no API token to create and nothing for your school to install.

On the website

The same planner with more room: the week grid, the assistant beside it, and keyboard shortcuts. When you sign up, it asks for your Canvas calendar feed link and, if you use one, a Google, Outlook or iCloud calendar link. Every due date then comes in on its own, on a phone, a Chromebook or any computer. The extension adds what the feed leaves out, such as points and whether you have already submitted.

Sign in with the same email in both and they show the same plan. To sign in, Cadence emails you a code or a link. There is no password.

Questions.

Is Cadence free?

Yes. There is no paid plan and there are no ads.

Does my school have to set anything up?

No. The extension reads Canvas through the login you already have in your browser, so there is no API token to create and nothing for an administrator to install.

Can I use it without Canvas?

Yes. Type an assignment in a sentence, like “Spanish quiz Thursday, 30 min”, and it is estimated and scheduled the same way. Activities and events never needed Canvas. The demo works the same way, with a sample week already in it.

How does it know how long something takes?

It starts from the assignment’s title and instructions. When you finish something it asks whether that took less time, about that, or longer, and later estimates for the same kind of work adjust. You can set the time yourself on any assignment.

What if I am having a bad day?

Tell the assistant how much you can do tonight. There is an example of that further up this page. The rest of tonight’s work moves to other days. If those days are full too, Cadence goes past your number or plans work after its due date, and tells you which, so you can choose.

Does it work on my phone?

Yes. The website works in a phone’s browser, and your Canvas assignments come in through your Canvas calendar feed link. The Chrome extension, which also reads points and what you have submitted, runs in Chrome on a computer or a Chromebook.

Who can see my plan?

Nobody, unless you add an accountability partner or join a club. They see your name and the check-ins for the activities you chose to share, and nothing about your assignments or your calendar. The privacy policy says exactly what they can see.

Is the assistant an AI?

On a copy of Cadence that offers it, yes: your messages go to Gemini, an AI model made by Google, which may use them to improve its products and may have people read them, so do not type anything private. It changes your plan through the same steps you could take yourself. With your own Anthropic key, Claude does the same. If neither can be reached, simple sentences are read by ordinary code on your own device. The privacy policy lists exactly what is sent.
