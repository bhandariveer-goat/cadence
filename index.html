    How it decides when you work

    Most planners start with a settings page that asks how many hours you want to work. Most students don’t know, and even a good answer is wrong on the Tuesday you have a meet. Cadence works out your hours from your calendar instead, using four rules.

    1. Free time is worked out one day at a time

      Each day runs from when you wake up to when you go to sleep. Cadence takes out school and everything on your calendar, and work can go anywhere in the time that’s left.

    2. Work goes on your lightest evenings first

      Cadence fills the emptiest evenings before the busy ones, and it only piles more onto an evening when something would otherwise miss its due date.

    3. What is due soonest goes first

      Cadence plans the most urgent work before anything else. Long assignments get split across several days, and you never get more than two blocks of the same thing back to back.

    4. Everything gets planned, even in a packed week

      When a week is too full, Cadence makes some days longer and, as a last resort, books work after its due date. It tells you which days and which assignments that affected, so you can decide what to drop, move or ask your teacher about.

    Use it on Canvas or on any screen

    The Chrome extension, on Canvas

    The extension adds a Cadence button to every Canvas page and reads your courses and assignments using the login you already have, so there’s no API token to create and nothing for your school to install.

    This website, on a phone or any computer

    The website is the same planner with more room, so you get the week grid with the assistant beside it, plus keyboard shortcuts. When you sign up, you can paste your Canvas calendar feed link and, if you use one, a Google, Outlook or iCloud calendar link, and from then on every due date comes in by itself. The extension adds what the feed leaves out, like points and whether you’ve already submitted something.

    If you sign in with the same email on both, they show the same plan. To make an account you enter your email, type in the code Cadence sends you and choose a password, and after that you sign in with just your email and password.

    Common questions

    Is Cadence free?

    Yes. There’s no paid plan and there are no ads.

    Does my school have to set anything up?

    No. The extension reads Canvas through the login you already have in your browser, so there’s no API token to create and nothing for an administrator to install.

    Can I use it without Canvas?

    Yes. Type an assignment the way you’d say it, like “Spanish quiz Thursday, 30 min”, and Cadence estimates and schedules it just like one from Canvas. Activities and events don’t need Canvas at all. The sample week doesn’t use Canvas either, and it comes with a week already filled in.

    How does it know how long something takes?

    It starts from the assignment’s title and instructions. When you finish something, it asks whether it took less time, about that long or longer, and uses your answer to adjust later estimates for the same kind of work. You can also set the time yourself on any assignment.

    What is in the app?

    There are five tabs. Today shows what to do next, how much is planned for the day, and a check-in for each activity once it’s over. Calendar has everything with a time on it, from school and practice to due dates and the blocks planned for your work. School lists every assignment with how long it should take and the steps to finish it, and each step is tied to the block it’s planned for. Activities covers things like practice, teams, clubs or a job, with a weekly count of whether you showed up. Crew is for accountability partners and clubs, who only see check-ins for the activities you choose to share. The sample week has all five.

    Can I just tell it what changed?

    Yes. Type it the way you’d say it, like “I just got home and I’m tired, an hour of work tonight”, and the assistant keeps tonight to that hour and moves the rest. If you mention something with a time, like dinner at 6:30, it goes on your calendar too. Each reply lists what changed and what moved because of it, and one tap undoes the whole thing. If you only say you’re tired without giving a number, it asks you one question and offers the answers as buttons.

    What if I am having a bad day?

    Tell the assistant how much you can do tonight, and the rest of tonight’s work moves to other days. If those days are already full, Cadence either goes past your number or plans work after its due date, and it tells you which one it did so you can choose what happens.

    Does it work on my phone?

    Yes. The website works in your phone’s browser, and your Canvas assignments come in through your Canvas calendar feed link. The Chrome extension, which can also read points and what you’ve submitted, runs in Chrome on a computer or a Chromebook.

    Who can see my plan?

    Nobody, unless you add an accountability partner or join a club. Even then, they only see your name and the check-ins for the activities you chose to share, and nothing about your assignments or your calendar. The privacy policy says exactly what they can see.

    Is the assistant an AI?

    On a copy of Cadence that offers it, yes. Your messages go to Gemini, an AI model made by Google, which may use them to improve its products and may have people read them, so don’t type anything private. The assistant changes your plan through the same steps you could take yourself, and if you add your own Anthropic key, Claude does the same job. If neither can be reached, ordinary code on your own device reads simple sentences. The privacy policy lists exactly what is sent.
