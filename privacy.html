Privacy policy

Last updated November 12, 2026

The short version. Cadence is a planner. Your plan is stored in your browser. If you sign in with an email, a copy is also saved to your account so your other devices can load it. Cadence does not show ads, does not track you across sites, records only anonymous use of the app (described below under "Anonymous usage data"), and does not sell or rent anything about you. A few features send data to other companies to work, and each one is listed below with exactly what is sent.

Who runs Cadence

Cadence is run by Veer Bhandari. For questions about this policy, or requests about your data, use the contact page or write to cadenceaiplanner@gmail.com.

What Cadence stores

In your browser

Everything in your plan is kept in your browser's storage (in the Chrome extension, the extension's own storage):

  • The name you gave Cadence.
  • Your assignments: titles, courses, due dates, instructions, points, and whether they are done.
  • Your activities, their schedules, and your check-ins, including any notes you write on them.
  • Calendar events you add, your school hours, roughly when you sleep, and any one-day changes you make.
  • What Cadence remembers about you: notes you or the assistant saved (such as "practice runs late on Tuesdays"), your planning preferences, and, unless you switch learning off, a record from the last six weeks of when you pressed Start on homework, how long timed blocks lasted, and which classes you moved later or finished. Cadence uses it to plan around how you actually work. You can see all of it, switch any of it off and delete it under What Cadence knows about you, in your account. Notes that look like an email address, a phone number, a link or a password are refused.
  • Your settings, including whether you chose the light or dark theme and whether you have dismissed the notice about storage.
  • When you confirmed you are 13 or older and agreed to the terms and this policy.
  • If you open an invite link to a club or a partner, its code, until you use it or for up to two weeks.
  • Your chats with the assistant: what you said and what it answered, up to 30 chats, and for the chat you have open, what the AI model needs to carry it on (each message to it includes the summary of your week described under "The assistant, and AI"). Chats are kept only in this browser, never in your account, until you delete them, sign out, or delete your data.
  • If you connect Gmail, or sign in with Google for Google Calendar: that it is connected and to which address. With Gmail, also the events Cadence found in your email, which emails it has read and which events you deleted, as described under "Gmail". Google's sign-in itself is never stored: it is held in memory while Cadence is open, and asked for again when it runs out.
  • If anonymous usage data is on: a random ID, and which counts have been sent. It is kept in its own storage slot, not in your plan, so it is never copied to your account. Records of how the app is used wait in memory until they are sent, and are not stored.
  • If you add them: an Anthropic API key, your Canvas calendar feed link, and a Google Calendar, Outlook or iCloud calendar link. These are never saved to your account, and leave your device only to reach the service they belong to, with two exceptions. On the website, your Canvas feed link goes to Cadence's server, which fetches your calendar from Canvas, hands it straight back to your browser, and keeps neither. And a Google Calendar, Outlook or iCloud link always goes to Cadence's server, which does the same with Google, Microsoft or Apple. (A browser does not let a website read another site's calendar itself. The Chrome extension can read Canvas, so for Canvas it does not use Cadence's server.) From a Google, Outlook or iCloud calendar, Cadence keeps only each event's title and time, on your device.

If you use Cadence without an email, this is the only place your plan exists. Clearing your browser's site data, or choosing Delete my data in your account, erases it.

In your account, if you sign in with an email

Signing in with an email is optional. If you do, Cadence stores with its database provider, Supabase:

  • Your email address and the name you chose.
  • A copy of your plan, as described above. The copy leaves out your Anthropic API key, your Google Calendar connection and its events, your Gmail connection and the events found in your email, your calendar links, and your chats with the assistant. Those stay on the device where you set them up.
  • If you use Crew: check-ins for the activities you chose to share, kudos, nudges, and which partners and clubs you are connected to.
  • A count of how many assistant requests your account made today, used only to enforce a daily limit. It is stored against an account ID, not your name or email. Old counts are deleted automatically, usually within a couple of days, and deleting your account does not remove a count early.

Each plan is stored in a row that only its owner can read or change. When you make an account, Cadence emails you a code to check the address is yours, and you choose a password. After that you sign in with your email and password, or ask for a code instead. That email is sent from a Gmail account that belongs to Cadence, so Google handles it on its way to you. Your password goes from your browser straight to Supabase, which keeps it only in hashed form (scrambled one way, so it cannot be turned back into the password). Cadence's own server never receives it, and Cadence never stores it.

Supabase runs the sign-in, and it keeps its own log of sign-ins: the time, the account, and the IP address and browser the sign-in came from. The person who runs Cadence can see that log.

Anonymous usage data

So that the person who runs Cadence can see whether people come back, where they get stuck and what they never find, Cadence records anonymously how it is used. Your browser makes a random ID, which is not your account, name or email, and sends these with it:

  • Counts: that you used Cadence on a day (at most once a day), that you finished setup, that your plan has an assignment or an activity in it, that you made your first check-in, or that you connected a calendar. Each of the last four is sent once.
  • How you use the app: which screen is open and for how long you use it (a screen left alone for a minute stops counting), which buttons and fields you press, named as they are in Cadence’s code (for example “tab:calendar” or “field:email”), presses on things that are not buttons, three or more quick presses on the same thing, how far you get in setup and the tour and whether you skip them, how long after agreeing to the terms you reached each count above, errors in the app (the kind of error and the place in Cadence’s code), and how long Cadence takes to open and to answer its own requests. These also carry a second random ID for the visit, which is new each time Cadence is opened.

Cadence’s server records the time each one arrives. Never sent: your name, your email, anything you type, what a field contains, the words on what you press, where on the screen you press, mouse movement or scrolling, your plan, titles, messages to the assistant, calendar contents, error messages, and nothing from Gmail or email: Cadence’s email screens and buttons are never recorded. No IP address, cookie or browser details are kept with any of it.

Before anyone signs up, the sign-up screen is counted with no ID at all: how many times a day it is shown, typed in, sent, and gets someone in. Nothing links those numbers to a person or a browser.

Only the person who runs Cadence can read these records, and only as totals: a page in the app that checks their sign-in turns them into counts and averages, and the database refuses to answer anyone else. No one can read one browser’s records with the public key.

All of this goes through Cadence’s server to Supabase (listed below). The counts are kept for up to 400 days, the records of how the app is used for up to 180 days, and the sign-up numbers for up to 400 days. The random ID lives in your browser in its own storage slot, not in your plan, so it is never copied to your account. Nothing with the ID is recorded in the demo, or until you have agreed to the terms and seen this policy. You can turn it off under Account, Anonymous usage data: that deletes the ID from your browser and asks Cadence’s server to delete everything recorded with it. Signing out, and Delete my data, do the same. If Cadence’s server cannot be reached when you do, what was recorded earlier, which holds only the random ID, stays until it is removed after 180 or 400 days.

If you write to us

The contact page has a form. When you send it, your message, the email address you give, and your name if you add one go to Netlify, the company that hosts this site. Along with them Netlify records the IP address and the browser the message was sent from, as it does for every form it receives. Netlify stores the message, checks it for spam with a filtering service called Akismet, and emails it to the person who runs Cadence, so a copy is also in their inbox.

It is used to answer you and for nothing else. It is not added to any list. It is not part of your plan and is not connected to it. Ask for a message to be deleted and it will be, in both places.

What Cadence reads from other services

Canvas

The Chrome extension reads Canvas using the Canvas login you already have in that browser. It reads your name, your courses, and your assignments (title, due date, instructions, rubric, points, submission status, and the items in the assignment's module). This is read by your own browser and saved into your plan. Cadence does not receive your Canvas password, does not create an API token, and cannot see anything in Canvas that you cannot see yourself. If you paste a Canvas calendar feed link, the Chrome extension fetches it itself; on the website, Cadence's server fetches it for you, hands it back and keeps nothing.

Google Calendar

Connecting Google Calendar is optional, and there are two ways. You can paste its secret iCal link, and Cadence's server fetches that calendar for you each time it updates, hands it back and keeps nothing; only each event's title and time are kept, on your device, and events marked free are left out. Or, in the Chrome extension, you can sign in with Google, and Cadence reads events from the calendars you pick so it can plan around them. Those events are kept on your device and are not copied to your account; the sign-in is held in memory only. Only if you separately turn it on, Cadence adds its own planned blocks to a calendar you choose, marks them as Cadence's, and never edits or deletes any event it did not create.

Gmail

Connecting Gmail is optional and works only in the Chrome extension. You sign in with Google and allow Cadence to read your email, using Google's read-only Gmail permission: Cadence cannot send, delete or change anything in Gmail. Once you connect it, and while "Add events from email automatically" is on, Cadence reads your recent email to find practices, games, deadlines and changes, and adds them to your calendar. It does this only while Cadence is open: when you open it, about every 30 minutes after that, and when you choose Check email now. Your browser reads your email straight from Google, without going through Cadence's server. It reads the text of emails in your inbox from the last two weeks, up to 25 at a time, and up to 3,000 characters of each; promotions, social and forum mail, spam, trash and drafts are left out. Attachments, images and links in an email are never opened, and text an email hides from view is left out where Cadence can tell.

The events are found on your computer. Where your computer can run it, Chrome's built-in AI model reads each email inside Chrome; otherwise Cadence matches dates, times and words like "practice" or "due" itself. Your email is not sent to Cadence or to any AI company. Cadence checks each event against its email: the sentence it came from must be in the email, and the date must match that sentence. An email that looks written to give instructions to an AI is skipped.

Events found in email are added to your calendar, labelled From email, with the sentence each came from. For each one, Cadence keeps its title, date and time, that sentence, the sender's name and email address, the email's subject, when it arrived and Gmail's id for it, in your browser's storage only: never in your account or on Cadence's server. It also keeps which emails it has already read (Gmail's id for each, not their contents) and which events you deleted, so that they are not added again. The assistant does not read your email, and the events found in it are left out of everything sent to the assistant's AI model. They are never added to your Google Calendar. If the Cadence website is open in the same browser, it can show these events by asking the Chrome extension directly, inside your browser, not through Cadence's server.

You can delete any event (open it on your calendar and choose Delete event), switch "Add events from email automatically" off, which stops the reading and hides the events, or disconnect Gmail. No one at Cadence reads your email, and Cadence's server keeps no record of it. The Gmail sign-in lasts about an hour and is kept only in memory while Cadence is open: it is never written to your device's storage or saved to your account. Disconnecting Gmail under Calendars forgets it straight away and removes the events found in your email from this device; when Google Calendar is not also connected, Cadence also asks Google to cancel the sign-in, and says so if Google does not confirm. You can remove Cadence's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions. While Google is reviewing Cadence's access to Gmail, only people Cadence has added as testers can connect it.

Cadence's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs, including Gmail and Google Calendar, will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. The use of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Google Calendar data is used only to show your calendar and plan around it, and Gmail data only to find the events added to your calendar. Neither is used for advertising or sold. Cadence does not use Gmail or any other Google user data to develop, improve or train AI or machine learning models, and does not send your email to any AI company. When the assistant uses an AI model, events that came from Google Calendar, either way, are passed on as a time with no title.

The assistant, and AI

On a copy of Cadence that offers it, every message you send the assistant goes to Gemini, an AI model made by Google, whether you use Cadence with an email or with only a name. If you add your own Anthropic key, it goes to Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, instead. Each time you send a message, the following is sent to that company to produce the reply:

  • The message you typed.
  • A summary of your week: the current date and time, your school hours and days off, your sleep times, any one-day changes you have made (such as a shorter evening), your open assignments (title, course, due date, time estimate), your activities with their schedules and how many times you have done each this week, your calendar events for the next seven days (title and time, or time only for events from Google Calendar, Outlook or iCloud), the work blocks already planned, and anything that does not fit.
  • What Cadence remembers about you: your saved notes, your planning preferences, and the patterns it has noticed (for example, that you usually start homework around 7:30pm). Not the raw record those patterns come from.
  • Earlier messages in the same chat.

The assistant does not read your email, and the events Cadence finds in your email are left out of the summary of your week and of everything else sent to the AI model.

If you type /secret, the assistant offers to outline an assignment: what it asks, the steps, the names of the parts you will hand in, what to look over first and questions to think about, and it is told never to give answers. Every line, and the whole outline, is kept short and lines that look like an answer are dropped, so it cannot write the work for you. For an outline, only that assignment is sent: its title, course, due date, time estimate and instructions. With no AI model, or with your own Anthropic key (outlines use Cadence's own assistant, not your key), the outline is made on your device from what Cadence already knows, with nothing sent.

This goes through Cadence's server, which adds Cadence's key and passes it on to Google. Cadence uses Google's free Gemini API. Under Google's terms for it, Google uses what it receives to improve its products, and people at Google may read it. So do not put anything in a message to the assistant that you would not want a stranger at a company to read: no health details, no passwords, and no private information about other people. If you would rather not send anything to Google, do not use the assistant: everything else in Cadence works without it. Cadence's server does not store your messages or the replies.

If you use Cadence with only a name, your browser first gets a hidden account from Supabase, Cadence's database provider, so that the server can count how many messages it sends in a day. The same hidden account is what lets Cadence's server fetch a calendar link for you. That account is a random ID and nothing else: no name, no email and none of your plan, and the database lets it do nothing but be counted. It is kept in your browser, and removed after 30 days without use.

If a reply from an AI model cannot be had, the assistant reads simple sentences on your own device instead, with nothing sent anywhere. With your own key, it goes straight from your browser to Anthropic under your Anthropic account.

With your own key, Cadence also sends the instructions of an assignment to Anthropic when it writes that assignment's summary, steps and time estimate, and, in the teacher tools, the text of an assignment you dictate.

How Google handles what it receives is described in the Gemini API terms and Google's privacy policy, and how Anthropic does in Anthropic's privacy policy.

If you use the microphone button, your browser turns your speech into text. In Chrome, that means the audio is sent to Google to be transcribed. Cadence receives only the text.

Cadence does not share your data with anyone else, and does not use advertising or third-party analytics services.

Cookies and tracking

Cadence does not set cookies and does not track you across sites. It uses your browser's storage to keep your plan, as described above.

A link to Cadence can carry a label saying where it was shared, for example utm_source=newsletter. Cadence removes that label from the address as soon as the page opens.

The label is not recorded or counted.

What other people can see

Nothing, unless you use Crew. Activities are shared unless you switch sharing off for one.

  • An accountability partner you add sees your name and your check-ins for every activity you share: the activity, how long, how it went, and any note you wrote.
  • A club or class you join sees your name, your check-ins for the activities you linked to that club (with the same details), and your progress toward its weekly goal. It does not see check-ins meant for another club, or for no club.

Neither can see your assignments, your calendar, or activities you do not share.

You can end a partnership or leave a club at any time from Crew, and from then on they no longer see your check-ins. A club's leader can also remove you from it.

Deleting your data

  • Without an email account, open your account in Cadence and choose Delete my data, or clear this site's data in your browser. Either also deletes your chats with the assistant. To delete one chat, open Your chats in the assistant.
  • With an email account, open your account in Cadence and choose Delete account. This erases your account, the saved plan, your check-ins, and your connections to partners and clubs, and removes your plan and your chats with the assistant from this browser. It cannot be undone. It does not erase entries already in Supabase's log of sign-ins.
  • To stop Cadence reading your email and remove the events it found, open Calendars in the Chrome extension and choose Disconnect on the Gmail card. To remove one event, open it on your calendar and choose Delete event. To take away Cadence's access to your Google account completely, remove Cadence at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • To stop anonymous usage data, open your account in Cadence and turn off Anonymous usage data. That deletes the random ID from your browser and asks Cadence’s server to delete everything recorded with it.
  • A message you sent from the contact page is not part of your plan, so neither of those removes it. Ask from the contact page and it is deleted, from Netlify and from the inbox it was sent on to.

To get a copy of your plan, open your account in Cadence and choose Download my data: it saves your plan, the same copy that is saved to an account, and your chats with the assistant, as a file. You can also ask for a copy of what is stored in your account, or ask for it to be deleted, from the contact page or by writing to cadenceaiplanner@gmail.com.

Age

Cadence is made for high-school students. It is not meant for children under 13, and children under 13 should not use it or sign in. If you are under 18, check with a parent or guardian before signing in with an email, and follow your school's rules about the tools you use for schoolwork.

Security

Data in your account is protected by database rules: only you can read or change your plan, and your check-ins reach only the partners and clubs described above. Keys for other services stay on your device, and Google's sign-ins are never stored. No system is perfectly secure, so avoid putting anything in a note or an event title that you would not want stored.

Changes to this policy

When this policy changes, the date at the top changes. If a change affects what is collected or who receives it, signed-in users will be told in the app.

The rules for using Cadence are in the terms of use.
